# Goal

Find companies where **fundamentals are improving faster than expectations are being revised** — and buy the gap before the market closes it.

The AI buildout is the current hunting ground because it is creating unusually large, unusually fast changes in real business economics. But the framework is about the *gap*, not the theme. If the AI trade dies and the gap opens somewhere else, the framework follows the gap.

---

## 1. The Core Claim

Prices reflect expectations. Returns come from **revisions to expectations**, not from good news per se.

So the question is never "is this a good company?" It is:

> **What is priced in, what is actually happening, and how long until those converge?**

A great company at a price that already assumes greatness is a bad idea. A mediocre company priced for collapse that merely stabilizes is a good idea. We are hunting for the *delta*, in either direction.

---

## 2. Where Our Edge Comes From (and Where It Doesn't)

Be explicit about this. If we can't name the edge on a given idea, we don't have one.

### Edges we can actually have

| Edge | Why it persists | How we exploit it |
|---|---|---|
| **Time arbitrage** | Institutions are judged quarterly and can't hold through a 6-month re-rating lag | Buy the muted reaction, hold across the digestion period |
| **Primary-source diligence** | Almost nobody reads the 10-Q language diff, the proxy comp metrics, or the 8-K exhibits | Read the filings, not the coverage of the filings |
| **Second-order supply chain** | The street models the obvious beneficiary; the picks-and-shovels two layers down get modeled late | Trace demand from the end customer backwards through the chain |
| **Cross-domain triangulation** | Analysts are siloed by sector; the memory analyst and the power analyst never talk | Use one sector's hard data to falsify another sector's narrative |
| **Narrative/fundamental divergence** | Sentiment moves faster than earnings; both overshoot | Detect when the story and the numbers point opposite directions |
| **Attention scarcity** | Sub-$10B names, foreign filers, post-SPAC, and recent spinoffs get thin coverage | Fish where the boats aren't |

### Edges we do NOT have — do not pretend otherwise

- Speed. We will never beat the tape on a headline. Anything that resolves in under a day is not our game.
- Private/expert-network information. We are working from public data.
- Macro forecasting. We can characterize the current regime; we cannot predict rates, oil, or elections. Treat macro as a *risk to size around*, never as a thesis.
- Binary events. Coin flips with no informational edge (single-trial FDA approvals, litigation verdicts) are gambling.
- Technical analysis. Price patterns are not evidence.

## 3. The Setups We Hunt

Each has a name so we can pattern-match and track hit rates per setup in `state/ledger.md`.

| Setup | Signature | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Muted Reaction** (ALAB) | Major positive surprise, stock does <½ the move the numbers justify | Market needs weeks to rebuild the model; the flat tape is the entry |
| **Fade the Pop** (DELL) | Big beat, initial pop, then a drift-down on profit-taking while estimates keep rising | Second entry at a better price with a de-risked thesis |
| **Slow Pivot** (DOCN) | Business mix shifting for 3+ quarters, still valued on the old mix | Re-rating is a multiple change, not just an EPS change — it's the biggest payoff |
| **Misclassified** (CEG) | Stock sold off because it got lumped into a bucket it doesn't belong in | Purely a labeling error; resolves when one print breaks the label |
| **Sentiment Overshoot** (MU) | Fundamentals reaffirmed or improved, stock down on rates/macro/vibes | Cleanest divergence — the numbers say one thing, the price says another |
| **Structural Break** | An industry rule changed (long-term contracts, regulation, capacity) so the old valuation model is wrong | Market keeps applying the historical cycle model to a business that no longer cycles the same way |
| **Orphan** | Post-spin, post-SPAC, foreign filer, or index-excluded — no natural owner | Forced/absent buyers create price dislocations unrelated to value |

If an idea doesn't map to one of these, either name a new setup and add it here, or be suspicious that there's no real thesis.

---

## 4. Disqualifiers — Hard Stops

An idea is dead regardless of how good the story is if any of these are true. Check these **first**; they save the most time.

- Going-concern language, or cash runway under 12 months with negative FCF and no committed financing
- Auditor resignation, material weakness, restatement, or an 8-K Item 4.02 (non-reliance on prior financials)
- Serial equity issuance funding operations (not growth) with no path to ROIC above cost of capital
- Revenue recognition that depends on related parties, circular deals (vendor financing its own customers), or non-cash barter
- Governance: dual-class with no sunset *plus* related-party transactions, or comp structures that pay out on metrics shareholders don't benefit from
- The entire thesis rests on a single customer or single contract with no diversification path
- We cannot articulate what would make us wrong

Circular AI deals deserve special scrutiny in this cycle: chip vendor invests in a startup, startup buys chips, vendor books revenue. Trace the cash.

## 5. Case Studies

Each case gets a lesson **and** a generalizable rule.

### DELL — Feb 26, 2026 (Fade the Pop)
- Feb 26 earnings showed AI server demand inflecting. Stock $120 → $140 (~17%).
- Subsequent quarters confirmed. Stock ran to $310–420. May 28, 2026: $43.8B revenue (+88%), AI server revenue $16.1B (+757%), FY27 AI server target raised $50B → $60B, backlog $51.3B.
- **Lesson:** The first print was the signal, and a 17% move was nowhere near a full re-rating of a 757% revenue trajectory.
- **Rule:** When a segment is growing triple digits, compare the *stock's* move to the *segment's* move, not to the stock's own history. A 17% move against a business inflection is a muted reaction.

### ALAB — May 5, 2026 (Muted Reaction)
- Very strong quarter; stock sat near $200. By May 29 it was $342 (~70% in 24 days).
- **Lesson:** Muted reaction to a genuinely strong print is a *timing* opportunity, not a verdict on quality.
- **Rule:** Before concluding "muted," verify the print was actually strong on the metrics that drive the model, not just on headline EPS. Then give it weeks, not days.

### DOCN — AI Orchestration Pivot (Slow Pivot)
- Mix shift visible from mid-2025 in conference commentary and product releases. May 5, 2026 earnings drove ~40%.
- **Lesson:** The pivot was observable for multiple quarters in qualitative sources before it showed up in the P&L.
- **Rule:** Product announcements, job postings, and conference language lead revenue by 2–4 quarters. Track them as leading indicators, not color.

### CEG — 2026 Double De-rating (Misclassified)
- Sold off both as a rate-sensitive utility *and* as an "AI infra" glut victim — two incompatible labels — while contracted cash flows improved and PJM demand hit records.
- **Lesson:** When a stock is being punished by two contradictory narratives at once, at least one is wrong and probably both.
- **Rule:** Explicitly ask "which bucket is this stock being traded in, and is that bucket correct?" Misclassification is one of the most reliable and least crowded setups.

### MU / Memory — July 2026 (Sentiment Overshoot + Structural Break)
- Fundamentals reaffirmed in the strongest terms available (sold out through 2027, non-cancelable LTAs to 2030, record margins) while the stock de-rated to ~13x forward on rates and cycle-reflex fear.
- **Lesson:** Two things were true at once — the divergence was real, *and* the bear's cyclicality argument was a legitimate structural question, not noise.
- **Rule:** A divergence is only tradeable if you can also explain *why* the other side is wrong. "The market is being irrational" is not an explanation. Here the actual argument is the LTA structure changing the cycle's shape — which is a testable claim.

### Anti-case: what we have NOT yet proven
We have identified divergences well. We have **not** yet demonstrated, with tracked outcomes, that we exit well or size well. `state/ledger.md` exists to find out. Until it has resolved entries, treat conviction language in reports as hypothesis, not track record.

## 6. What The System Must Do

1. **Establish what's priced in** before forming a view. No thesis without an expectations baseline.
2. **Detect change from primary sources** — filings, transcripts, regulatory data — not from summaries of them.
3. **Measure divergence** between fundamentals, positioning, sentiment, and price.
4. **Argue against itself** via an adversarial council before assigning conviction.
5. **Commit to falsifiable predictions** with probabilities and dates.
6. **Size and set exits** — an idea without an invalidation trigger isn't an idea.
7. **Score itself** and change the framework when it's wrong.

## 7. What The System Must NOT Do

- Chase a move that already happened and reverse-engineer a reason
- Treat sell-side price targets as evidence of value (they are evidence of *consensus*, which is useful for a different purpose)
- Let a good narrative substitute for numbers with dates attached
- Report social sentiment as fact
- Claim to have checked a source it could not access
- Recommend "watch" as a way of avoiding a decision — if it's a watch, name the specific trigger that changes it
