# SNOW — Snowflake

**Dossier opened 2026-09-11.** First baseline. Watchlist tier: New → propose T2 Delta.
**Confidence: High — the central question was resolved at T1 from the 10-Q and two 10-Qs of comparatives.**

## The one-line verdict

The declining RPO that looked like an early warning is **contract-duration shortening, not customers failing to re-commit** — neutral to mildly bullish. The cost is that the thesis now rests on observed consumption momentum rather than contracted visibility.

---

## 1. The RPO question, resolved

RPO fell while revenue accelerated and estimates rose. Four hypotheses were tested; **B (duration shortening) is dominant** and C and D are contradicted.

**The decisive T1 disclosure, which appears only in 10-Q prose:** the weighted-average term of **newly signed** capacity contracts collapsed from **~3.1 years (FY2026) → 2.2 (FQ1 FY27) → 2.1 years (FQ2 FY27)**. RPO is a total-contract-value measure, so the same annualised dollar books roughly a third less.

**Decomposition kills the bear reading outright.** Splitting RPO into deferred revenue plus uninvoiced non-cancellable backlog:

| As of | RPO | Deferred revenue | Uninvoiced backlog |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-31 | $9,771.5M | $3,361.4M | $6,410.1M |
| 2026-04-30 | $9,205.2M | $2,877.5M | $6,327.7M |
| 2026-07-31 | $9,004.3M | $2,596.2M | $6,408.1M |

Deferred revenue fell $765.2M against an RPO fall of $767.2M, so the **uninvoiced backlog moved $2.0M — essentially nil**. In the April→July quarter, the one hardest to explain away, the uninvoiced backlog **rose $80.4M** while total RPO fell $200.9M. That decline is consumption of capacity already paid for at the January renewal peak, not lost commitments.

**Corroboration from the other side:** the share of RPO expected within twelve months rose **46% → 50% → 54%**, so derived current-portion RPO grew to ~**$4,862.3M, +40.3% YoY** (management said ~42% on the call) while the non-current portion **fell 21.5%** from January. Current +40.3% against non-current +19.5% is the textbook duration-shortening signature.

**Implied bookings:** raw H1 is **−3.6% YoY** ($2,170.5M vs $2,251.2M). Normalised for term — $2,170.5M ÷ 2.1 = $1,033.6M against $2,251.2M ÷ 3.0 = $750.4M — they are **+37.7%**, matching 37% product revenue growth. FQ2 alone +38.3%; FQ1 weaker at +21.8%.

**C and D contradicted:** NRR rose **125% → 126%** (bottomed at 124%); the $1M cohort grew **26.6% to 828**; total customers reached **14,554** with net new +32% YoY; the **Observe and Natoma acquisitions enlarged** the perimeter during the quarters RPO fell; and the RPO definition is **unchanged word-for-word across five filings**.

## 2. What changed

- FY2027 product revenue guidance **raised twice: $5,660M (+27%) → $5,840M (+31%) → $6,070M (+36%)** — 900bp of growth-rate upgrade in six months.
- Non-GAAP operating margin guidance rose in parallel, **12.5% → 13.5% → 14.5%**.
- Operating leverage is real on **both** bases: GAAP net loss **−12.4% of revenue vs −26.0%** a year ago; GAAP operating margin −17.0% vs −29.7%; **SBC 28.1% of revenue vs 35.8%** (34.1% FY26, 40.8% FY25). Non-GAAP operating margin 15.3% against GAAP −17.0% — a **32-point basis gap**, which they flag explicitly.
- Forward contractual coverage **improved**: current-portion RPO covers **89.5% of TTM revenue, up from 84.2%**, and 80.1% of guided FY27 product revenue.

## 3. The information edge

Management addressed RPO **proactively in prepared remarks** and gave the reconciliation itself. Across **eleven analysts, not one asked about RPO, backlog, or contract duration.** And management did **not** volunteer the 3.1 → 2.1-year term change on either call — it exists only in 10-Q text.

The most important number about the quarter is buried in filing prose, absent from the call, and unasked. That is a direct **Information Edge** input.

## 4. The honest cost

Duration shortening is **customer-driven and macro-linked by Snowflake's own account**: customers "rationalize budgets, prioritize cash flow management, **including through shortened contract duration**." Customers are committing the same or more annualised dollars over materially shorter horizons. So the position rests more on observed consumption and less on contracted backlog than two quarters ago. Direction of travel unchanged; **asymmetry changed.**

## 5. Falsifiable test → candidate prediction (Phase 6)

Whether the weighted-average new-contract term **stabilises at or above ~2.1 years through the FQ4 renewal peak**, and the twelve-month recognition share **stays at or above 50%**. Rationale: if duration keeps falling while that share keeps rising, two effects currently netting out to "fine" will stop netting out — there is a floor under contract length and none under the multiple.

## 6. ⚠ Metric restatement — second instance of the P-007 pattern
