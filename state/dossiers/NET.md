## 2. 🛑 The split trap is ARMED on this name and has not sprung

**Cloudflare has a stockholder-approved corporate action the company itself says will halve the price, and it may land this month.** This is not a conventional split. The "Capitalization Changes" create non-voting **Class C** stock and Series FF preferred, exchange co-founder Class B shares, and then execute the **"Class C Split"**: each Class A share "would be reconstituted and become one share of Class A common stock and one share of Class C common stock." Verbatim (T1, Q2 2026 10-Q):

> "we believe that the market price for the shares of our Class A common stock will generally reflect the effect of a **two-for-one stock split** and, accordingly, the market price of the Class A common stock will decrease by **approximately one-half**."
> "At the 2026 Annual Meeting, our stockholders approved the elements … we anticipate implementing the Capitalization Changes **as early as September 2026**."

**Not implemented as of 2026-09-11**, on three independent checks: EDGAR's last 8-K is 2026-08-13 with no Form 8-A or charter amendment since; the price series is continuous with no halving; and litigation is pending — **eight stockholder complaints** as of 2026-08-05, seven consolidated alleging breach of fiduciary duty because the changes "allegedly allow the Co-Founders to maintain control and retain significant liquidity opportunities," an eighth alleging a two-thirds vote was required and not sought.

**This is worse than the CRWD case, not better.** Our declared panel states verbatim: *"This stock does not have any record of stock splits,"* Last Split Date "n/a." So it has never restated a prior estimate for this name — and the coming event is not a clean ratio change but a **share reconstitution creating a second listed line**, which also adjusts the conversion rate on $5.79bn of converts. Every failure mode that produced "15 cuts, 0 raises" on CRWD is present, plus a novel one.

## 4. The implied path — the crux

EV ~$108.58bn is **37.9x FY2026E revenue** and **29.3x FY2027E**; trailing price to free cash flow is **319x**.

To justify the price with zero return at a 25x exit on FCF needs $4,366M of FCF (109,149 ÷ 25). At a 30% FCF margin — more than double today's 13.6% — that needs **$14,553M of revenue, 5.08x FY2026 guidance**: 26.1% compound for seven years, or 38.4% for five. On a gentler 30x exit and 35% margin, $10,395M, 3.63x, or 20.2% for seven years.

Consensus three-year growth of 29.78% **clears the 26.1% requirement** — but only if growth persists four years past the forecast horizon **and** the FCF margin roughly doubles while SBC runs at 23% of revenue. **That is the crux: the price is not obviously wrong, but it needs both halves, and nothing current shows the margin half happening.** Non-GAAP operating margin was flat YoY, and the panel forecasts FY2026 FCF of $348.60M — *down* from $383.34M in FY2025.

## 7. Transmission Check

**Who writes the cheque: all three, explicitly, and the split between them is never quantified (T1, Q2 10-Q).**

1. **Developer with a credit card** — pay-as-you-go via the website, Pro and Business priced **per registered domain**, "with a credit card on a monthly or annual basis."
2. **Enterprise procurement** — contracts "typically range from one to three years," tailored and mostly non-cancelable.
3. **Platform team with a budget line** — "**'pool of funds'** in which the customer commits to spend at least a specified amount … do not require the customer to subscribe for specific products or spend any specific amounts during any month, quarter or … year," but funds must be used in the period. A pre-committed budget drawn down on consumption.
4. **Free tier** as funnel — "efficient brand marketing" and "virtual quality assurance."

**Pricing model: both, and disclosed qualitatively.** The security leg is **seat-priced** — Zero Trust "available **on a per seat basis**." The developer leg is **consumption-priced** — "a **usage-based plan that is metered by requests and execution time**."

**So NET straddles the exact axis the enterprise-software thesis is built on**, with both legs inside one segment and **neither quantified**. If AI agents compress seats, Zero Trust is exposed; if AI workload grows, the metered platform benefits mechanically. **NET cannot be placed on billing model at all — only on multiple and duration.**

⚠ One constraint on how large the AI consumption leg can be: the revenue policy says "**Usage-based consideration is primarily related to fees charged for our customer's use of excess bandwidth**." But this sentence is **verbatim unchanged from the FY2025 10-K**, as are both pricing passages — so it is carried-forward boilerplate describing a stable architecture, not a current measurement of mix. Suggestive, not dispositive.

**Repriceable by something that isn't a number: yes** — it moved 10.51% in a session with no filing and no identifiable cause. **Caps Catalyst Path at 3.**

## 8. 🛑 Metric withdrawal — candidate THIRD instance, triggers mandatory promotion

The FY2025 10-K (2026-02-26) disclosed **paying customers: 189,791 (2023) → 237,714 (2024) → 332,466 (2025)** — a 39.9% jump in the final disclosed year — alongside the >$100,000 cohort. Both 2026 10-Qs then state:

> "**Prior to 2026, our key business metrics included paying customers** … **We have ceased including this metric** because it has become significantly less important to us over time."

**The finding is the tension.** In the Q2 press release of **2026-08-06** — the same day the 10-Q repeated that the metric was dropped as no longer important — the CEO said the quarter was "highlighted by revenue accelerating … and **record growth in total paying customers**, large customers, and developers on our platform." **Management is claiming record growth in a metric it stopped publishing, in the same filing package, with no number attached.** "Developers on our platform" is likewise cited and has never been disclosed at all.

The *discontinuation* was **footnoted with a stated reason** — better conduct than the Salesforce or Snowflake cases. What is unsupported is the affirmative growth claim about the withdrawn series.

**How it differs from the two logged instances:** Salesforce *widened* Agentforce ARR in the quarter it reported it; Snowflake *restated* the same Forbes count four times. Cloudflare **withdrew**. The mechanism is identical in effect — a series the market used to track growth is no longer checkable while management continues to assert the growth — so if the rule is about *auditability of disclosed metrics*, withdrawal belongs. **Retro decision required; if accepted this is instance three and `framework/improve.md` mandates a change.**

**No other redefinition:** the $100,000 threshold, the DBNR definition, the Annualized Revenue calculation and the single-segment presentation are all unchanged. One conventional, disclosed addition — "restructuring and other charges" joined the non-GAAP exclusions in Q2 2026.
