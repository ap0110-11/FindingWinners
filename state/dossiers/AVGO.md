# AVGO — Broadcom

**Dossier opened 2026-09-11.** First baseline. Watchlist tier: New → propose T1 Deep.

## The one-line verdict

The disclosure gap *is* the thesis question: an enormous contracted backlog concentrated in a single channel, with **zero customer attribution**, at a valuation that assumes the counterparties are creditworthy hyperscalers. Whether that gap is an edge or a trap is the council's job.

## 1. What changed

The 10-Q falsifies it: a 50bp rate move **"would not impact the interest expense on our borrowings outstanding."** Debt fell **$67.1bn → $61.1bn**, net debt is **2.0% of market cap**, and interest expense is *falling*, $807M → $778M. I had propagated "acquisition" into two lane briefs; both lanes killed it independently at T1. Any screen or summary describing AVGO as mid-acquisition is wrong.

## 2. Concentration — established, and it inverts a comparison I got backwards

Figures and as-of dates live in the watchlist entry under **"✅ Concentration — established 2026-09-11"**; that block is the authority and is not duplicated here. The structural reading:

🛑 **Corrected 2026-09-11 — I had misread the filing three ways, all in the same direction of inventing a second data point where there was only a year-over-year comparison.** The verbatim 10-Q language (T1, filed 2026-09-10) is: *"Direct sales to one semiconductor solutions customer, which is a distributor, accounted for 50% and 46% of our net revenue for the fiscal quarter and three fiscal quarters ended August 2, 2026, respectively, and 32% and 30% … for the fiscal quarter and three fiscal quarters ended August 3, 2025."*

- **One distributor is 50% of FQ3 net revenue, up from 32% for the same quarter a year earlier** — 46% and 30% on a three-quarter basis. ❌ The 32% is **the same distributor a year earlier, not a second distributor.** There is **no second-largest-customer disclosure at all.**
- ❌ **"All-distributor share rose to 56% from 46%" is not a time series** — both figures are the *same* period. All distributors together were **56%** of net revenue for the three quarters ended 2026-08-02, and this single distributor was **46%** of those same three quarters. The correct reading: **every other distributor on earth combined is about ten points.** (The watchlist entry already had this right; the error was mine in restating it.)
- **End**-customer concentration: top five ~**55%** for the quarter and ~**50%** for three quarters, against ~**40%** for both periods a year earlier. ❌ The 40% is the **prior-year top-five, not a top-one figure. No top-one end-customer number is disclosed anywhere.** The only top-one figure in the filing is the 50% distributor, which is a *channel* number.
- The distributor is **channel in form, demand in substance** — roughly **71% of the segment** and **~89% of AI semiconductor revenue** flows through it.
- The counterparty is almost certainly **WT Microelectronics**. ⚠ **High-confidence inference, not a disclosure.** The FQ3 FY2026 10-Q contains **zero** occurrences of "WT Micro" and names no end customer for any part of the RPO; the inference rests on the Q1 FY2024 10-Q naming plus matching prior-year comparatives. **Nothing in the 2026-09-10 filings advances it**, and Taiwanese exchange filings were not reached — so it is unresolved, not refuted.

🛑 **Correction to my own earlier read.** I told the user AVGO's 50% was "worse on a top-one basis than Credo's 74% top-three." That compares a *channel* figure to an *end-demand* figure — not comparable. Like-for-like: AVGO top-five **end** customers ~55% against **CRDO's 74% top-three / 84% top-four**. **CRDO is materially more concentrated**, and CRDO's is *improving* (88% → 84% → 74%) while AVGO's deteriorates. This is why the compute-buildout theme row now says CRDO is the more concentrated name like-for-like.

**🛑 Disclosure asymmetry — and the absence is worse than "not disclosed."** CRDO discloses receivables concentration (85% in two customers). AVGO's 10-Q contains **zero occurrences of "concentration of credit risk"**, no percentage and no customer count; the nine uses of "receivable" are the balance-sheet line (trade receivables $13,707M against $7,145M at FY-end), the cash-flow line, contract-asset definitions, and a **factoring** note.

That factoring note is the finding: **$1,600M of receivables were sold in the quarter and $3,950M year to date.** Selling receivables **mechanically removes concentrated balances from the balance sheet before anyone could measure them.** So the concentration is not merely unmeasured — the measurement base is actively being reduced. Put alongside a customer that is 50% of revenue and unnamed, this is the single most adverse disclosure fact on the name.

## 3. RPO — the crux

**Confirmed at T1 on 2026-09-11, and the attribution language is explicit** (10-Q filed 2026-09-10): *"the remaining performance obligations under these contracts as of August 2, 2026 were approximately **$179.2 billion**. These commitments include obligations under a **long-term contract for custom AI accelerators entered in the fiscal quarter ended May 3, 2026**. We expect approximately **25%** of this amount to be recognized as revenue over the next 12 months."*

The FQ2 entry date matches the **+$119.6bn step** in the series, so the **~$120bn single-contract inference stands** — as an inference, with the company confirming that a single custom-AI-accelerator contract entered that quarter is inside the total. Duration extension confirmed: the twelve-month share fell **49% → 25%**.

Longer-dated backlog is ordinarily a quality signal. Here it compounds the problem, because a longer contract with an unnamed counterparty reached through a distributor that is half of revenue means **the credit and concentration risk is both larger and less observable**.

**🎯 Council crux:** *a ~$120bn single-contract RPO sitting behind 50% single-channel concentration with zero customer attribution.* The bull reading is contracted multi-year AI demand from a hyperscaler that cannot realistically default. The bear reading is that we cannot verify the counterparty, the tenor, the cancellation terms, or whether the distributor bears any credit risk — and the disclosure regime does not require AVGO to tell us. Both readings fit every disclosed fact, which is precisely the condition the council exists for.

### 🛑 The revision counter is broken on FY2027 — and AVGO has no split, so this is a *different* defect

Nasdaq's panel reports **FY Oct-2027 at 5 up / 7 down (n=13)** while **the same vendor's own quarterly rows for the four quarters composing that year sum to 14 up / 3 down** (Oct-26 5/3, Jan-27 3/3, Apr-27 4/0, Jul-27 4/0). Annual and quarterly panels draw on different analyst subsets so they need not match — but **they cannot legitimately carry opposite signs.** Current year is clean: FY Oct-2026 **10 up / 2 down**.

**Corroboration settles the sign as positive.** (a) *Level:* consensus Q4 FY2026 revenue of $34,881.6M sits **0.24% above** the company's ~$34.8bn guide — consensus is chasing guidance upward. (b) *Guidance action, T1:* the 2026-09-02 release guided Q4 revenue +93% YoY and AI semiconductor revenue from $16.7bn to **$21.7bn (+236%)**, with non-GAAP operating margin held at 66% — an up-guide on every line. **Discard the annual counter and use the trajectory.**

**This generalises our CRWD lesson.** AVGO's last split was 2024-07-15, **26 months ago and outside the window** — confirmed three ways (vendor split record, zero occurrences of "split" in the 10-Q, FINRA `stockSplitFlag` empty on all four settlements from 2026-06-30). So the split mechanism has no purchase here. **The failure was never really about splits; it was about trusting a count instead of a trajectory.**

**90 days is not separately establishable, and structurally cannot differ.** AVGO's prior print was the 8-K of 2026-06-03, **100 days** before 2026-09-11 and therefore outside the 90-day window — so exactly one guidance event anchors both windows.
