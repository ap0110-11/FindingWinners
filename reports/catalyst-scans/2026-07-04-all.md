## The Overbuilding Narrative — What Actually Happened

On **July 1**, Bloomberg reported that Meta is building **"Meta Compute"** — a cloud business to resell its excess AI infrastructure/GPU capacity to third parties (similar to what SpaceX started doing this year). The market read this as the first real crack in the "compute is perpetually scarce" thesis that has underpinned AI hardware valuations.

**The reaction (July 1-2):**
- Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX): **-6.3% July 1, another -5.4% July 2** (~-10% in two days)
- Meta itself: **+9-10%** (market applauded monetizing idle capacity)
- Memory: MU -10% (erased ~$138B market cap in a session), SNDK -10%
- Neoclouds: CoreWeave -14%, NBIS -17% intraday, IREN -6.5% (then -45% peak-to-trough with an idiosyncratic scandal, see below)
- Asia follow-through: SK Hynix -14.6%, Samsung -9% on July 2

**The critical counter-signal — the power layer says the opposite of a glut:**
- **PJM hit a record 166.3 GW of demand** (July 2), surpassing its 2006 record, and warned of an "imminent electricity reliability emergency"
- Day-ahead power ran from $44 to **$436/MWh (+~900%)**, with the 7-8pm peak above $1,200/MWh near the Virginia data center hub
- Frontline developers (Digital Realty, etc.) confirm **ERCOT interconnection is now the binding constraint** — you can't even get studied

### The key framing for the basket

The overbuilding fear hits **compute suppliers and compute resellers** — the businesses whose valuations assume perpetual scarcity of chips/GPUs. It does **not** hit **power** (the actual bottleneck) or **demand-side AI software** (which benefits from cheaper compute). This splits our watchlist into three very different buckets:

| Bucket | Overbuilding impact | Watchlist names |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------|
| **Compute supply / resellers** | NEGATIVE — repriced if scarcity ends | MU, SNDK, MRVL, ALAB, CRDO, NBIS, DOCN, IREN, BRUN, AMD, NVDA |
| **Power (the real bottleneck)** | POSITIVE — glut fear is misdirected; power is scarce | CEG, VST, BE, VRT* |
| **Demand-side AI software** | NEUTRAL-to-POSITIVE — cheaper compute helps | CRM, NOW, RBRK, CRWD, PANW |

\*VRT is a hybrid — it sells into the buildout (so exposed to a capex pause) but benefits from rising density/power intensity.

**Bottom line for the user's question:** The selloff created the sharpest dislocations in the compute-supply names, but the *highest-quality* undervalued opportunities are now in the **power bucket** (CEG, VST) — where the narrative is actively *wrong* — and the **demand-side software** names (CRM, RBRK) — which are insulated from the glut entirely. Among compute resellers, IREN is the most complicated: cheap, but now carrying both narrative risk and a self-inflicted governance wound.

## Detailed Scans — Undervalued Names (User Focus)

### CEG — The Overbuilding Fear Is Pointed at the Wrong Layer
- **Score: 21/25** (Info: 4 | Quality: 5 | Valuation: 5 | Catalyst: 4 | Sentiment: 3) — *up from 19/25*
- **Bucket:** Power | **Mismatch: YES**
- **What's New:** Stock at ~$239 (down from ~$257 on June 8), near the low end of its 52-week range ($228-412). The pullback is driven by (1) a share overhang — CEG is trading ~16-17% below the ~$279-281 price of its June block buyback/offering, and (2) indiscriminate "AI infrastructure" sector selling. Meanwhile the fundamentals got *stronger*: PJM hit a record 166.3 GW demand on July 2 with day-ahead prices spiking ~900% (to $436/MWh, peaks >$1,000/MWh near the Virginia data center hub). PJM has been fast-tracking data center deals.
- **Why the overbuilding narrative is misdirected here:** The Meta Compute story is about *compute* oversupply. CEG sells *power* — the one resource that is verifiably scarce. Every gigawatt of data center capacity (built by anyone, including Meta) needs firm, carbon-free baseload. CEG owns ~25% of US nuclear capacity with signed 20-year PPAs (Microsoft/Crane, Meta/Clinton). Those contracts don't care whether Meta resells compute.
- **Variant Perception:** Market is treating CEG as an "AI beneficiary" that rises and falls with chip sentiment. It's actually a regulated-ish power oligopolist with multi-decade contracted cash flows. At ~$239 vs median PT $358 (+50%) and P/E ~22x on $11-12 FY26 EPS, the risk/reward is skewed up.
- **Upcoming Catalysts:** Q2 earnings Aug 6. PJM colocation framework to FERC (ongoing). Share overhang clearing. Crane restart milestones.
- **Bear Case:** Block-share overhang persists near-term. PJM colocation rules could still limit direct-to-DC contracts. Rate/regulatory risk on nuclear lifecycle costs. Rich vs traditional utilities.
- **Recommendation:** Add to portfolio / increase. The best risk/reward on the watchlist right now — an undervalued name where the dominant market narrative is actively wrong. Use the overhang-driven weakness. Watch Aug 6 earnings.
