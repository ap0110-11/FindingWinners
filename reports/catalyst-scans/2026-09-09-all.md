## 1. Scan Questions & Answers

**Q1. Is the July "sentiment overshoot, not demand deterioration" call confirmed or falsified?**
**Confirmed, decisively, and now measurable.** Semiconductors fell 29% from June 22 to July 29 — the fastest correction since 2000 — with the low marked by a hedge-fund margin call during the third-largest three-day deleveraging on record. During that same stretch, Q2 semiconductor earnings expectations *rose* from +126% to +144% year over year. Prices collapsed while estimates climbed eighteen points. Hyperscaler cloud growth accelerated (Google Cloud +82%, Azure +43%, AWS +37%), foundry revenue set a record at $53.49B, and memory shortage forecasts extended through 2028. Selling paid nothing; holding paid everything.

**Q2. What is the regime now, and does it still support long-duration AI exposure?**
It supports it more than the headline suggests, for a specific technical reason. The Fed is priced ~60% to *hike* on September 16 and the 10-year sits at 4.836%, but **the 10-year real yield rose only ~5bp (2.36%→2.41%) while the 2-year real yield went from 0.51% to 2.08%.** The discount-rate damage is at the front of the curve; the terminal-value math driving long-duration equities is far less impaired than a three-year-high nominal yield implies. Credit confirms it — high-yield spreads at 268bp, below the 10th percentile of their 20-year range. The exception is genuine: AI-related bonds are ~4% of the high-yield index but ~40% of 2026 net new issuance, and data-center spreads have been *widening* since June while broad high yield tightened. Financing cost, not demand, is where this regime does real damage.

**Q3. Where has the expectations gap actually opened, now that we measure it properly?**
In one place we expected and one we did not. **CRDO fell 29.1% while its forward estimates rose 3.7%** — roughly 35 points of pure de-rating with zero estimate deterioration, taking it to 23.0x against its own 38.7–112.7x history. That is the cleanest divergence in the scan. The surprise is the inverse case: **DELL is the only one of eight names where the visible consensus trajectory misses what the price requires**, needing 22.7% annual EPS growth against consensus at 13.7%, with implied durable EPS at 137% of the furthest-out estimate. Dell executed superbly and the price got there first.

**Q4. Did the July bucket-level calls hold?**
Three of them inverted, with one shared root cause. Defense was hurt rather than helped (appropriations stalled into continuing resolutions; primes down 13–30%). Power lagged despite record demand (utilities +0.47% over three months; duration governs). Demand-side software went from safe harbour to most exposed (GPT-6 Astra on Sept 4). All three failures come from **treating a sector's operational exposure as its market exposure**, which is now a mandatory check in `framework/scoring.md`.

## 3. Regime

**Hawkish repricing with an energy shock and benign credit — and the damage is at the wrong end of the curve to hurt terminal values.** The Fed is priced ~60% to hike September 16, Brent is back above $100 (+25% since July 13), and the 10-year is at a three-year high, yet the S&P and Nasdaq are both up ~2.1% and high-yield spreads sit below the 10th percentile of their 20-year range.

**Helps:** compute supply and memory, where demand data improved *through* the drawdown and the GPT-6 Astra launch rotated capital toward data-center capex. Behind-the-meter power equipment (BE, VRT), which gets more valuable as states mandate bring-your-own-generation.

**Hurts:** neoclouds, and this is the one bucket with genuine fundamental deterioration — a financing channel, not a demand channel. Demand-side software, which flipped from safe harbour to most-exposed on a competitor's model release. Defense, where the binding constraint is appropriations rather than conflict.

**Leaves alone:** ASTS, which has no transmission to AI capex, memory pricing, power prices, or defense appropriations.

**Discount-rate versus demand, stated explicitly.** Discount-rate driven and carrying no fundamental information: the entire June 22–July 29 semiconductor drawdown, the utilities de-rating, and most of the long-end nominal yield move (real rates rose 5bp; the rest is breakevens and term premium, partly from $1.5T of AI-related issuance crowding the curve). Demand driven and carrying real information: hyperscaler cloud acceleration, the memory shortage extension through 2028, record foundry revenue, power as a confirmed physical bottleneck, the neocloud credit widening, and — genuinely unresolved — the software competitive position after Astra.

### Council — CRDO, 2026-09-09

**Crux:** Is the 64.5% gross margin (from 68.2%) evidence of price commoditization at the top-two customers, or of a deliberate mix shift into lower-rate, higher-dollar-content optics?

At FY26's 51% non-GAAP net margin that is roughly $1.19B of net income against a 23.0x forward multiple.
Key evidence: T1 Sept 1 beat-and-raise, revenue +114.7%; T4 estimates +3.7% over 30 days with 5 raises / 1 cut against a −32.8% price move; T2 short interest flat at 6.01M → 6.13M through the entire decline. Kill: FY27 growth guidance cut below 60% by the FQ3 print in late March 2027.

At the guided run-rate a single top-two account is roughly $640M annualized and about $410M of gross profit against FY26 non-GAAP net income of $662M, with $88M of quarterly stock compensation and largely fixed opex — so one design-out is a **greater than 60% earnings cut**, not a growth adjustment. ### Council — MU, 2026-09-09

**Crux:** Are the HBM long-term agreements price-committed or volume-only? Everything turns on this. If they fix price, the contracted-supply mechanism is real and the cycle's shape has changed. If they only commit volume at prevailing market prices, then 86% gross margins are a spot-price spike wearing a contract's clothing.

**Bull (mechanism):** FQ4 is guided to $50B ± $1B at ~86% gross margin — an $8.5B sequential revenue step where roughly $7.3B lands as gross profit against an opex base that does not scale with DRAM pricing. HBM3E/HBM4 is fully booked through CY2027 on non-cancelable agreements running to 2030, so FQ1 and FQ2 revenue is contracted rather than forecast. HBM consumes roughly three times the wafer area per bit of standard DRAM and is sold on qualification-gated multi-year agreements, so the marginal wafer is absorbed into a contracted channel rather than dumped into spot — the mechanism that historically ended memory cycles has no channel to operate through.
Key evidence: T1 FQ3 revenue $41.46B +346% with the FQ4 guide; T4 consensus +22.0% EPS and +15.8% revenue over a genuine 84-day measurement; T3/T4 Samsung HBM4 past $1B in four months. Kill: FQ4 guides FQ1 revenue below $52.0B or gross margin below 82%.

**Bear (mechanism):** The audited ASC 606 backlog is **$5B — one-tenth of the cited "$100bn"**, which is a minimum-volume construct and not an accounting obligation. At ~86% gross margin, incremental bits are nearly pure profit, which is the definition of a price spike rather than a structural margin. A 30% ASP reversion against flat volumes puts essentially the whole revenue decline into gross profit — on a ~$200B base that is roughly $40 of EPS in a single year of normalization.
Key evidence: T1 $5B audited backlog; T1 ~86% guided gross margin; T4 FY27 estimate range $130.90–$220.21, the widest relative dispersion in the group. Kill: the FY2026 10-K discloses audited RPO above $40B by Dec 31, 2026.

**The bear's most important correction, which changes the ranking:** implied durable EPS at a 12x exit is $85.65, which is **117% of the FY Aug-2026 consensus of $73.40** even though it is only 54% of the FY27 consensus of $158.01. Both are true. The market is paying for roughly FY26 earnings in perpetuity, not for a collapse — which is a materially less dramatic gap than the "6.5x forward" headline implies. I have marked Expectations Gap down accordingly.

Rowe +165.3%, Coatue +1,794%, Situational Awareness from 17,362 to 4,828,786 shares. The base is turning over toward faster hands. Marginal buyer if bull right: the three long-only houses that just sold ~20.6M shares, the largest identifiable reservoir. Marginal seller if bear right: the ~16.6M shares accumulated in one quarter by price-sensitive hands, which will be sold faster than they were bought.

## 12. Process Notes

**Which lanes produced the decisive evidence.** The filings lane produced the single most valuable finds in the scan and it was not close: Sandisk's $31.3bn footnote, Salesforce's $2.53-per-share gain disclosure, IREN's grace periods and 88%-deferred cash flow, Dell's financing-receivable add-back, and AVAV's $80M-against-$500M obligation rate. The positioning lane produced the single best *structural* insight, VST's absence of a marginal buyer, and it caught a data contamination that would have corrupted the scan silently.

**What was slow or low-yield.** The sentiment lane lost four of its six specified sources and had to improvise; what survived was valuable, but the ratio of effort to yield was the worst of the six. The macro lane was high-yield but is a single lane for the whole scan and should stay that way.

**Framework changes made during this scan.** Four, each triggered by evidence rather than preference:
1. `framework/scoring.md` — **the Transmission Check**, added after three simultaneous instances of treating operational exposure as market exposure. The three-instance rule fired.
2. `skills/catalyst-scan.md` — **brief dates must be copied from the ledger or dossier**, added after four wrong dates across two briefs, one of which produced a false finding in a downstream lane.
3. `framework/sources.md` Lane B — **sanity-check 13F aggregates against shares outstanding before use**, and prefer EDGAR Form 4 XML over screen-scraped insider data.
4. `framework/sources.md` Lanes C and D — **the price reaction adjudicates conflicting consensus panels**, and a rewritten access section reflecting which sentiment sources actually work.

**What should change next.** Three things.

First, **acquire borrow-cost data.** It was the most valuable missing input in two of eight council debates and it has no substitute.

Second, **pull option chains for every gated name, not two.** Implied-versus-realized is the most direct read on what is priced, and it was missing for six of eight — including DELL and CRDO, where dispersion is narrowest and the question matters most.

Third, and most important: **the council format worked, and the isolation is why.** Both opening seats failed their own reversal tests on names where the evidence did not support a thesis, and they did so unprompted — the Bull surrendered DELL, VST and AVAV; the Bear surrendered MU, SNDK, CRDO and VST. Where both surrendered the same name (VST), the correct answer turned out to be "neither side has a thesis," which is exactly the verdict the framework says should be reached more often than high conviction. The bear's ex-gain correction on CRM moved that name down a full rank and changed its conviction tier. That is a debate adding value rather than performing one. **Retain the isolated-openings requirement even when it is expensive.**

One honest caution to carry into the next retro: five of eight gated names are one position. The correlation analysis found that a single hyperscaler-capex shock moves **six of eight in the same direction**, with CRM moving opposite and AVAV not moving at all. A second, different shock — credit spreads widening — reorders the group entirely, hitting IREN, VST and DELL while leaving MU and SNDK comparatively untouched. Which shock arrives determines whether this book behaves as one position or three, and the sizing must reflect the theme, not the ticker.
